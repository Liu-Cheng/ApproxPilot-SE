\begin{abstract}
Approximate computing improves accelerator power, performance, and area (PPA) by replacing various arithmetic units with the most appropriate approximate implementations while preserving application-level output quality. However, exploring the resulting combinatorial design space requires costly evaluations of the resulting hardware designs. Surrogate models enable efficient design space exploration (DSE) by predicting hardware cost and application quality without evaluating every candidate. Building accurate surrogates, however, often requires substantial labeled data for pretraining or training a separate model for each target accelerator. Acquiring these data incurs considerable evaluation time, even when performed offline.

This paper presents ApproxPilot-SE, a self-evolving DSE framework that begins exploration with surrogate models trained on a limited initial dataset. To support search before the models become sufficiently accurate, ApproxPilot-SE retrieves similar, previously measured configurations and uses their prediction residuals to calibrate latency estimates. A path-aware readout further captures circuit dependencies for latency prediction. During DSE, a Pareto-front-guided feedback mechanism selects promising configurations for logic synthesis and application-quality evaluation. The resulting measurements expand the training dataset and calibration reference set, enabling the surrogate models to evolve with successive DSE iterations and guide subsequent search.

On a discrete cosine transform (DCT) accelerator, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.79 times the verified PPA hypervolume gain of the stronger baseline at 90\% structural similarity index (SSIM) retention with 720 training labels per method. With only 540 labels, it achieves 3.42 times the best baseline gain obtained with 900 labels, using 40\% fewer training labels. These results demonstrate improved approximate-accelerator optimization with fewer target-specific training samples.
\end{abstract}
