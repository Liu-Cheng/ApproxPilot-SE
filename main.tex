\author{Yanzhe~Wang,

        Fangfa~Fu,
        Bing~Yang
        and~Jinxiang~Wang}

\begin{abstract}
Approximate computing typically improves accelerator power, performance, and area (PPA) by replacing selected arithmetic units with approximate implementations while preserving application-level output quality. However, exploring the large combinatorial design space requires costly system-level evaluations. Existing GNN-based approaches rely on substantial training data collected either for each target accelerator or for pretraining, making data acquisition a major cost of surrogate-assisted optimization. We present ApproxPilot-SE, a self-evolving design space exploration framework that progressively adapts its surrogate models through search-guided evaluation. Starting from a limited labeled dataset, ApproxPilot-SE jointly explores approximation-site selection and arithmetic-unit assignment, selects Pareto-relevant configurations for logic synthesis and application-level evaluation, and incorporates the resulting PPA and quality measurements into subsequent surrogate training. This feedback loop couples model learning with design exploration, directing the evaluation budget toward regions relevant to optimization instead of relying on a surrogate trained once before search. A path-aware delay readout and local calibration using measured configurations further support system-level delay estimation. On a discrete cosine transform (DCT) accelerator, ApproxPilot-SE outperforms ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN across five structural similarity index (SSIM) retention thresholds, using 720 training configurations versus 900 for each baseline. At 90\% retention, its verified three-dimensional PPA hypervolume gain is 3.81 times that of the stronger baseline. At 99\% retention, it provides additional PPA hypervolume beyond the exact-design reference, while both baselines provide zero gain. A representative DCT design retains 99.62\% of the exact-design SSIM while reducing area, power, and delay by 19.36\%, 20.68\%, and 9.70\%, respectively. These results demonstrate improved verified hardware tradeoffs with fewer target-system training labels.
\end{abstract}
