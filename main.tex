\title{ApproxPilot-SE: Co-Evolving Surrogate Models and DSE for Approximate Accelerators}

\author{Yanzhe~Wang,
        Cheng~Liu,
        Fangfa~Fu,
        Bing~Yang
        and~Jinxiang~Wang}

\maketitle






\begin{abstract}
Approximate computing improves the power, performance, and area (PPA) of hardware accelerators while maintaining acceptable application accuracy. However, the large design space and costly system-level evaluations make efficient design space exploration (DSE) challenging. Existing surrogate-based methods also require substantial training data to predict PPA and accuracy.

This paper presents ApproxPilot-SE, a framework that enables surrogate models and DSE to co-evolve through iterative feedback. Graph neural networks (GNNs) are used to predict PPA and application accuracy. During DSE, Pareto-relevant configurations are selected for logic synthesis and accuracy evaluation. The measured results are then used to update the surrogate models and guide subsequent search.

To improve latency prediction, we introduce a local latency calibration method. It uses nearby measured configurations to correct prediction errors and updates the calibration references as new data become available. A path-aware readout further captures circuit dependencies for latency prediction.

Experiments on the discrete cosine transform (DCT) benchmark show the optimization performance of ApproxPilot-SE. At 90\% structural similarity index (SSIM) retention, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.79 times the PPA hypervolume gain of the stronger baseline with 720 training labels per method. With only 540 labels, it achieves 3.42 times the best baseline gain obtained with 900 labels, using 40\% fewer training labels.
\end{abstract}
