\begin{abstract}
Approximate computing improves accelerator power, performance, and area (PPA) by replacing various arithmetic units with the most appropriate approximate implementations while preserving application-level output quality. However, exploring the resulting combinatorial design space requires costly evaluations of the resulting hardware designs. Surrogate models enable efficient design space exploration (DSE) by predicting hardware cost and application quality without evaluating every candidate. Building accurate surrogates, however, often requires substantial labeled data for pretraining or training a separate model for each target accelerator. Acquiring these data incurs considerable evaluation time, even when performed offline. This paper presents ApproxPilot-SE, a self-evolving DSE framework that begins exploration with surrogate models trained on a limited initial dataset. To support search before the models become sufficiently accurate, ApproxPilot-SE retrieves similar, previously measured configurations and uses their prediction residuals to calibrate latency estimates. During DSE, promising configurations on the predicted Pareto front are selected for logic synthesis and application-quality evaluation. These measurements verify the hardware cost and output quality of important candidate designs, reducing reliance on potentially biased surrogate predictions. The evaluated configurations are then incorporated into the training dataset and calibration reference set to refine subsequent predictions. This feedback enables DSE and surrogate learning to co-evolve, progressively improving the exploration of approximate accelerator designs. Across five accelerator benchmarks and five structural similarity index (SSIM) retention requirements, ApproxPilot-SE achieves the highest verified PPA relative hypervolume in 24 of 25 settings with 720 training configurations per method. At 90\% SSIM retention, it improves verified PPA relative hypervolume by a factor of 2.30 relative to the strongest baseline per benchmark (geometric mean across five benchmarks). These results demonstrate improved verified hardware tradeoffs across different accelerator structures and application-quality requirements.
\end{abstract}
