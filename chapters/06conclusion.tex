
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we proposed ApproxPilot-SE, an automated approximate accelerator optimization framework. The framework couples multiobjective design space exploration with Pareto-guided acquisition of measured configurations and iterative surrogate retraining. It employs configuration-aware GNNs to predict hardware PPA and application quality, while incorporating path-aware readout and measured-reference local calibration for latency estimation. By integrating newly acquired measurements into both surrogate training and latency calibration, ApproxPilot-SE enables model learning and design exploration to evolve together, improving the utilization of limited ground-truth evaluations.

Experiments on five domain-specific accelerators demonstrate the effectiveness of ApproxPilot-SE across different circuit structures and application-quality requirements. Using 720 training configurations per method, ApproxPilot-SE achieves the highest or equally highest relative PPA HV in 24 of 25 benchmark--quality settings. At 90\% SSIM retention, it achieves a relative HV of 13.66$\times$ on DCT, compared with 4.34$\times$ for the strongest baseline. The data-efficiency experiments further show that ApproxPilot-SE rapidly reaches a high optimization level with limited training data. With only 512 training configurations, it achieves a relative HV of 11.82$\times$, exceeding the 7.05$\times$ obtained by ApproxPilot with 8192 configurations.

Further optimization analysis examines the contributions of iterative feedback, Pareto-guided selection, and configuration-aware latency estimation. The results demonstrate the benefits of incorporating newly measured configurations into surrogate updates, selecting optimization-relevant candidates for ground-truth evaluation, and exploiting circuit-path information together with local latency calibration. Together, these mechanisms enable ApproxPilot-SE to achieve competitive PPA--accuracy tradeoffs across diverse approximate accelerator design spaces with improved utilization of ground-truth evaluation data.
