
\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

In this section, we evaluate ApproxPilot-SE for approximate accelerator design and compare it with ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot,vlcek2025approxgnn}. We first examine the optimization results across different accelerators, including larger designs with substantially more configurable arithmetic sites. We then evaluate end-to-end data efficiency and investigate the effectiveness of iterative feedback, feedback selection, and latency modeling.













\subsection{Experimental Setups}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks:}
We evaluate ApproxPilot-SE on five domain-specific accelerators: DCT, Laplace filtering, Sobel edge detection, Gaussian filtering, and KMeans clustering. Their arithmetic operations and configurable sites are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}.

The DCT accelerator contains 80 arithmetic operations, including 76 configurable sites and four fixed exact operations. Laplace contains 60 configurable sites, while Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans contain 5, 17, and 16 sites, respectively. The initial design spaces of DCT and Laplace reach approximately $3.26\times10^{91}$ and $1.77\times10^{72}$ configurations. Compared with the accelerators evaluated in the original ApproxPilot framework~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot}, these larger accelerator structures substantially expand the combinatorial design space and present greater challenges for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Approximate operator library:}
The arithmetic-unit library combines EvoApproxLib components and CGP-generated approximate units. Each configurable site is assigned compatible arithmetic implementations according to operation type, bit width, signedness, and interface requirements. The resulting accelerator configurations exhibit different hardware costs and numerical error characteristics, providing the design space for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation platform:}
Hardware area, power, and latency are obtained using Synopsys Design Compiler with a 45-nm technology library. Application quality is evaluated using the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM)~\cite{wang2004ssim} on image-processing benchmarks, with evaluation images drawn from BSDS500~\cite{arbelaez2011contour}. Accuracy retention is calculated relative to the fully exact implementation.

\noindent\textbf{Framework settings:}
ApproxPilot-SE employs separate GNN models for PPA and application accuracy prediction. The PPA encoder contains four layers, while the accuracy encoder uses five layers. Both models have a hidden dimension of 128. NSGA-III is employed for multi-objective design space exploration, and local latency calibration uses a maximum Hamming distance of $d_{\max}=10$.








ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN serve as the baseline methods, retaining their respective prediction models and search strategies. To evaluate data efficiency, we consider training data sizes of 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, and 8192 labeled configurations. After optimization, each method selects 100 final candidates for independent ground-truth PPA and application-quality evaluation.




\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metric:}
We employ relative three-dimensional PPA hypervolume (HV) to evaluate the optimization results under different SSIM-retention constraints. Area, power, and latency are normalized by the corresponding values of the exact accelerator. With reference point $\mathbf{r}$, relative HV is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{HV}_{\mathrm{rel}}
    =
    \frac{
        \mathrm{HV}(\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
        \cup\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})
    }{
        \mathrm{HV}(\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})
    },
    \label{eq:relative_hv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}=(1,1,1)$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\tau}$ contains the measured PPA points satisfying the SSIM-retention threshold $\tau$. The reference point is set to $\mathbf{r}=(1.1,1.1,1.7)$ based on the observed distribution of normalized PPA values, with a larger margin for latency to accommodate its wider variation. A larger relative HV indicates better combined area, power, and latency tradeoffs. All reported HV values are computed from ground-truth-verified designs.

















\subsection{Overall Approximation Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization_results}

We first compare ApproxPilot-SE with ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN across five accelerators under SSIM-retention requirements ranging from 80\% to 99\%. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain}, ApproxPilot-SE achieves the highest or equally highest verified relative PPA HV in 24 of the 25 evaluated cases, demonstrating its effectiveness across different accelerator structures and quality constraints.

The advantages are particularly evident on the larger DCT and Laplace benchmarks. At 90\% SSIM retention, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.15$\times$ and 2.29$\times$ the relative HV of the strongest baseline on DCT and Laplace, respectively. 

As the SSIM-retention requirement becomes more stringent, the feasible design space becomes increasingly restricted, making it more challenging to obtain favorable hardware tradeoffs. Nevertheless, ApproxPilot-SE maintains a substantial advantage in the high-accuracy region. On DCT, its relative HV advantage over the strongest baseline increases from 2.54$\times$ at 80\% retention to 3.59$\times$ at 95\% retention. Even at 99\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE maintains a clear advantage and achieves the highest relative HV on all five benchmarks. These results highlight its ability to identify hardware-efficient configurations while preserving high application accuracy.

The advantage is particularly evident in the high-SSIM region, where ApproxPilot-SE continues to identify favorable PPA--quality tradeoffs while preserving application accuracy. These observations show that ApproxPilot-SE improves both the overall quality of the explored design set and the available hardware tradeoffs under stringent accuracy requirements.













\noindent\textbf{End-to-End Data Efficiency:}
The effectiveness of surrogate-assisted DSE depends not only on optimization quality but also on the amount of ground-truth data required to obtain competitive designs. To evaluate data efficiency, we compare ApproxPilot-SE, ApproxPilot, and ApproxGNN on DCT with training data sizes of 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, and 8192 labeled configurations. Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency} reports the relative HV under a 90\% SSIM-retention constraint, computed from ground-truth-verified final designs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/DCT_Data_Efficiency.pdf}
\caption{End-to-end data efficiency on DCT under 90\% SSIM retention. Relative HV is computed from ground-truth-verified final designs.}
\label{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}
\end{figure}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}, ApproxPilot-SE reaches a high level of optimization quality with a small training dataset. Its relative HV increases from 11.82$\times$ at 512 configurations to 14.36$\times$ at 1024 configurations and remains at a comparably high level as the training data size increases, reaching 15.77$\times$ at 8192 configurations. In contrast, ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN show overall improvements with larger training datasets, but neither reaches the optimization level achieved by ApproxPilot-SE across the evaluated training data sizes.

Comparing different training data sizes further highlights this difference in data efficiency. With only 512 training configurations, ApproxPilot-SE achieves a relative HV of 11.82$\times$, exceeding the 7.05$\times$ and 5.01$\times$ obtained by ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN with 8192 configurations, respectively. Thus, ApproxPilot-SE achieves higher verified optimization quality using 16 times fewer training labels. These results indicate that coupling surrogate updates with design exploration enables the framework to reach competitive PPA tradeoffs with substantially fewer ground-truth measurements.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime_breakdown}, data acquisition dominates the total runtime, accounting for approximately 86\%--93\% across the three frameworks. ApproxPilot-SE maintains a runtime comparable to ApproxPilot and substantially lower than ApproxGNN. Combined with the data-efficiency results, this demonstrates that ApproxPilot-SE achieves higher optimization quality without introducing substantial additional runtime overhead.













\subsection{Optimization Analysis}
\label{sec:optimization_analysis}

The effectiveness of ApproxPilot-SE depends on both surrogate prediction accuracy and how ground-truth measurements are utilized during exploration. To investigate the contributions of its key mechanisms, we conduct comparative experiments across five benchmarks, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:optimization_analysis}.

\noindent\textbf{Iterative Feedback and Pareto-guided Selection:}
Limited initial training data may not adequately cover the high-dimensional design space, restricting the effectiveness of fixed surrogate models. ApproxPilot-SE addresses this limitation by incorporating newly measured configurations into surrogate training, allowing subsequent exploration to benefit from accumulated design information. Compared with one-shot training, iterative feedback increases the relative HV on KMeans from 23.37$\times$ to 39.63$\times$, demonstrating the benefit of continuously refining the surrogates during exploration.

The effectiveness of this feedback process also depends on how the limited ground-truth evaluation data is allocated. ApproxPilot-SE prioritizes Pareto-relevant candidates and configurations near the accuracy constraint boundary, directing new measurements toward regions relevant to optimization. Compared with random selection, this strategy increases the relative HV on Sobel from 11.45$\times$ to 16.06$\times$ and achieves higher optimization quality on DCT and KMeans. Together, iterative feedback and Pareto-guided selection enable the search and surrogate models to improve through successive rounds of exploration and measurement.

\noindent\textbf{Latency Modeling:}
Beyond the effective use of measured data, accurate hardware-cost estimation is essential for guiding design space exploration. Latency depends on computational dependencies and changes in critical paths, making its prediction sensitive to circuit configurations. ApproxPilot-SE employs local latency calibration to correct surrogate estimates using prediction residuals from nearby measured configurations. Without this mechanism, the relative HV decreases from 13.66$\times$ to 11.48$\times$ on DCT and from 33.49$\times$ to 30.12$\times$ on Gaussian. The complete framework achieves higher relative HV on four of the five benchmarks, indicating that local calibration improves candidate evaluation and supports more effective system-level optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Parameter Sensitivity:}
We further examine the effects of the accuracy-boundary sampling ratio and feedback schedule on DCT. Varying the boundary sampling ratio from 0\% to 40\% yields relative HV values of 13.81$\times$--14.20$\times$. The feedback schedules of $45$, $90$, and $180$ achieve 13.11$\times$, 13.81$\times$, and 13.47$\times$, respectively. These results indicate that ApproxPilot-SE maintains competitive optimization quality across different parameter settings.
