
\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We compare ApproxPilot-SE with ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot,vlcek2025approxgnn} across five accelerators. We first assess verified optimization results at a fixed training budget, then examine end-to-end data and runtime efficiency across training budgets. Finally, we isolate the contributions of the optimization mechanisms, assess latency prediction accuracy, and study parameter sensitivity.













\subsection{Experimental Setups}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks:}
We evaluate ApproxPilot-SE on five domain-specific accelerators: DCT, Laplace filtering, Sobel edge detection, Gaussian filtering, and KMeans clustering. Their arithmetic operations and configurable sites are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}.

The DCT accelerator contains 80 arithmetic operations, including 76 configurable sites and four fixed exact operations. Laplace contains 60 configurable sites, while Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans contain 5, 17, and 16 sites, respectively. The initial design spaces of DCT and Laplace reach approximately $3.26\times10^{91}$ and $1.77\times10^{72}$ configurations. Compared with the accelerators evaluated in the original ApproxPilot framework~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot}, these larger accelerator structures substantially expand the combinatorial design space and present greater challenges for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Approximate operator library:}
The arithmetic-unit library combines EvoApproxLib components and CGP-generated approximate units. Each configurable site is assigned compatible arithmetic implementations according to operation type, bit width, signedness, and interface requirements. The resulting accelerator configurations exhibit different hardware costs and numerical error characteristics, providing the design space for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation platform:}
Hardware area, power, and latency are obtained using Synopsys Design Compiler with a 45-nm technology library. Application quality is evaluated using the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM)~\cite{wang2004ssim} on image-processing benchmarks, with evaluation images drawn from BSDS500~\cite{arbelaez2011contour}. Accuracy retention is calculated relative to the fully exact implementation.

\noindent\textbf{Framework settings:}
ApproxPilot-SE employs separate GNN models for PPA and application accuracy prediction. The PPA encoder contains four layers, while the accuracy encoder uses five layers. Both models have a hidden dimension of 128. NSGA-III is employed for multi-objective design space exploration, and local latency calibration uses a maximum Hamming distance of $d_{\max}=10$.








ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN serve as the baselines, retaining their respective prediction models and search strategies. After optimization, each method selects 100 final candidates for independent ground-truth PPA and application-quality evaluation.




\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metric:}
We employ relative three-dimensional PPA hypervolume (HV) to evaluate the optimization results under different SSIM-retention constraints. Area, power, and latency are normalized by the corresponding values of the exact accelerator. With reference point $\mathbf{r}$, relative HV is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{HV}_{\mathrm{rel}}
    =
    \frac{
        \mathrm{HV}(\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
        \cup\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})
    }{
        \mathrm{HV}(\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})
    },
    \label{eq:relative_hv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}=(1,1,1)$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\tau}$ contains the measured PPA points satisfying the SSIM-retention threshold $\tau$. The reference point is set to $\mathbf{r}=(1.1,1.1,1.7)$ based on the observed distribution of normalized PPA values, with a larger margin for latency to accommodate its wider variation. A larger relative HV indicates better combined area, power, and latency tradeoffs. All reported HV values are computed from ground-truth-verified designs.

\subsection{Overall Approximation Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization_results}


\subsubsection{Comparison Protocol}
Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain} compares the three methods on the five benchmarks in Section~\ref{sec:setup} at a matched budget of 720 labeled training configurations per method and benchmark. ApproxPilot-SE divides this budget between $n_0$ initial configurations and batches of $b_t$ newly measured configurations acquired over $R$ feedback rounds, so that $n_0+\sum_{t=1}^{R} b_t=720$. Specifically, $n_0=540$, $R=2$, and $b_1=b_2=90$. In each feedback round, 18 configurations are allocated to accuracy-boundary sampling, while the remaining 72 are selected through Pareto-guided acquisition. The new labels update both the surrogates and the calibration references.

For the baselines, the comparison counts target configurations used for training or adaptation; any source-domain pretraining is accounted for separately. ApproxPilot uses randomly sampled target configurations for surrogate training. ApproxGNN uses the other four benchmarks for source-domain pretraining and adapts its pretrained model to the target benchmark using the matched target-label budget. Each method uses an additional set of 60 validation configurations, disjoint from the 720 training configurations. Each method performs 15,000 surrogate-guided trials in the final search. The final 100 candidates per method are evaluated independently and are not counted as training labels. Verified relative HV is evaluated at 80\%, 85\%, 90\%, 95\%, and 99\% SSIM retention using the exact design as the reference.



\subsubsection{Verified PPA Trade-offs}
\noindent\textit{Comparison with exact designs:}
At 80\% SSIM retention, all three methods improve on the exact-only reference on all five benchmarks. ApproxPilot-SE attains relative HV between 15.50$\times$ on DCT and 238.74$\times$ on KMeans. As the required retention increases, fewer measured designs remain feasible and the achievable HV falls. This effect is especially strong for KMeans, whose relative HV for ApproxPilot-SE falls from 238.74$\times$ at 80\% retention to 1.59$\times$ at 99\%. At 99\%, however, ApproxPilot-SE still exceeds the exact-only reference on all five benchmarks. It retains the largest relative HV on Laplace (10.72$\times$) and DCT (10.25$\times$), followed by Gaussian (7.46$\times$), Sobel (4.06$\times$), and KMeans (1.59$\times$). A value of 1.00$\times$ indicates that the verified candidates add no PPA hypervolume beyond the exact design.

\noindent\textit{Comparison with prior methods:}
Across the 25 benchmark--threshold pairs, the geometric mean of the relative-HV ratios is 2.61$\times$ against ApproxPilot and 2.79$\times$ against ApproxGNN. The maximum ratios are 24.96$\times$ against ApproxPilot on KMeans at 85\% retention and 13.53$\times$ against ApproxGNN on KMeans at 90\% retention. Taking the stronger baseline separately for each pair gives a geometric mean of 2.09$\times$ and a maximum of 13.53$\times$ (KMeans at 90\%: 39.63$\times$ versus 2.93$\times$). ApproxPilot-SE leads outright in 23 of 25 pairs, ties ApproxGNN on Sobel at 80\%, and trails ApproxPilot only on Sobel at 99\%. It leads on all five benchmarks at each threshold from 85\% through 95\%. The gains vary by workload: on Sobel they are generally smaller, whereas KMeans shows a large advantage at intermediate thresholds and DCT and Gaussian show their largest advantages at 99\%. The large KMeans ratio at 90\% reflects both the PPA trade-offs found by ApproxPilot-SE and the small HV achieved by both baselines at that threshold.

\noindent\textit{DCT under tighter accuracy constraints:}
On DCT at 90\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE reaches 13.81$\times$ the exact-design HV, compared with 4.34$\times$ for ApproxPilot and 3.95$\times$ for ApproxGNN. This is 3.18$\times$ the stronger baseline. At 99\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE still reaches 10.25$\times$ the exact-design HV and 8.99$\times$ the stronger baseline. The latter ratio partly reflects that both baselines are close to the exact-only reference. The DCT ablations in Section~\ref{sec:optimization_analysis} show that removing iterative feedback, guided selection, or local latency calibration reduces final HV at 90\% retention. These results support the contribution of those mechanisms on DCT; the comparison alone does not isolate their effects at 99\%.






\subsubsection{DCT Pareto Frontiers}
To show the trade-offs behind the aggregate HV results, Fig.~\ref{fig:measured_pareto} plots the measured DCT candidates and their two-dimensional Pareto fronts for area, power, and latency versus SSIM retention. Each hardware metric is normalized to the exact design, so values below 1 indicate a reduction.




The area and power projections show that ApproxPilot-SE can retain hardware savings over a range of quality levels, including near the exact design's SSIM. The latency projection likewise includes several ApproxPilot-SE candidates below the exact-design latency at high retention, where many baseline candidates remain at or above that reference. The scatter also shows that not every candidate improves every objective. These projections illustrate individual trade-offs within the final measured sets; Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain} evaluates area, power, and latency jointly under each SSIM constraint.













\subsection{End-to-End Data and Runtime Efficiency}
\label{sec:data_runtime_efficiency}

\subsubsection{End-to-End Data Efficiency}
The effectiveness of surrogate-assisted DSE depends not only on optimization quality but also on the amount of ground-truth data required to obtain competitive designs. To evaluate data efficiency, we compare ApproxPilot-SE, ApproxPilot, and ApproxGNN on DCT with training data sizes of 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, and 8192 labeled configurations. Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency} reports the relative HV under a 90\% SSIM-retention constraint, computed from ground-truth-verified final designs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{figures/Fig3_DCT_Data_Efficiency.pdf}
\caption{End-to-end data efficiency on DCT under 90\% SSIM retention. Relative HV is computed from ground-truth-verified final designs.}
\label{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}
\end{figure}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}, ApproxPilot-SE reaches a high level of optimization quality with a small training dataset. Its relative HV increases from 11.82$\times$ at 512 configurations to 14.36$\times$ at 1024 configurations and remains at a comparably high level as the training data size increases, reaching 15.77$\times$ at 8192 configurations. In contrast, ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN show overall improvements with larger training datasets, but neither reaches the optimization level achieved by ApproxPilot-SE across the evaluated training data sizes.

Comparing different training data sizes further highlights this difference in data efficiency. With 512 training configurations, ApproxPilot-SE achieves a relative HV of 11.82$\times$, exceeding the 7.05$\times$ and 5.01$\times$ obtained by ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN with 8192 configurations, respectively. Thus, ApproxPilot-SE achieves higher verified optimization quality using one-sixteenth as many training labels in this DCT comparison.

\subsection{Optimization Analysis}
\label{sec:optimization_analysis}


\subsubsection{End-to-End Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:optimization_analysis} compares the full framework at 90\% SSIM retention with three variants: one-shot surrogate training, random feedback selection, and search without local latency calibration. Relative HV measures each mechanism's contribution to the final verified design set. All variants use a total budget of 720 labeled target configurations and independently evaluate 100 final candidates. The full framework starts with 540 configurations and acquires 90 additional labels in each of two feedback rounds.  The one-shot variant instead obtains 720 randomly sampled target training configurations, and trains its surrogates once using the corresponding training split. Random Selection retains the same feedback schedule but replaces Pareto-guided acquisition with random sampling, while No Calibration disables local latency calibration.

Compared with one-shot training, iterative feedback improves relative HV on three of five benchmarks, with the largest absolute increase on KMeans (23.37$\times$ to 39.63$\times$). Guided selection outperforms random selection on DCT, Sobel, and KMeans; on Sobel it raises relative HV from 11.45$\times$ to 16.06$\times$. Local calibration improves final relative HV on four of five benchmarks, including DCT (11.48$\times$ to 13.81$\times$) and Gaussian (30.12$\times$ to 33.49$\times$). Some variants perform better on individual benchmarks, so the benefit of each mechanism depends on the workload. These results assess design selection quality; they do not directly measure latency prediction error.

\subsubsection{Latency Prediction Accuracy}

To measure the effects of the readout and calibration directly, we separate latency prediction from end-to-end DSE. Table~\ref{tab:latency_prediction_ablation} compares conventional graph-level pooling with the path-aware readout, each with and without local calibration. All four variants are evaluated on the same 60 validation configurations, which are disjoint from the 720 training configurations, using MAE, RMSE, and Spearman rank correlation.


Local calibration reduces prediction error for both readout mechanisms. For graph-level pooling, calibration decreases MAE from 0.2490 ns to 0.2075 ns and improves Spearman correlation from 0.8569 to 0.8711. For the path-aware readout, calibration reduces MAE from 0.2761 ns to 0.1964 ns and RMSE from 0.3393 ns to 0.2732 ns. The calibrated path-aware model achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE among all four variants, reducing MAE by 5.35\% compared with calibrated graph-level pooling. Its Spearman correlation of 0.8683 is also close to the highest value of 0.8711. These results show that combining path-aware representations with local latency calibration provides the most accurate absolute latency estimates among the evaluated variants while maintaining strong candidate-ranking performance.

\subsubsection{Parameter Sensitivity}
We examine two choices that control feedback on DCT: the fraction of evaluations allocated to the predicted accuracy boundary and the number of newly evaluated configurations per feedback round. Fig.~\ref{fig:feedback_sensitivity} shows the results under a fixed budget of 720 training configurations. Varying the boundary-sampling ratio from 0\% to 40\% yields relative HV values of 13.94$\times$, 13.81$\times$, and 14.20$\times$ at 0\%, 20\%, and 40\%, respectively. The relatively small differences indicate that the optimization quality is not highly sensitive to the boundary-sampling ratio within the evaluated range.

 For the feedback schedule, the initial training pool contains 540 configurations, and the remaining 180 evaluations are distributed across feedback rounds. The additional 60 validation configurations are excluded from this budget. Batch sizes of 45, 90, and 180 correspond to four, two, and one feedback rounds, respectively. The batch size of 90 achieves the highest relative HV (13.81$\times$), compared with 13.11$\times$ at 45 and 13.47$\times$ at 180. These results indicate that an intermediate feedback batch size provides a favorable balance between the frequency of surrogate updates and the number of new measurements acquired per round.
