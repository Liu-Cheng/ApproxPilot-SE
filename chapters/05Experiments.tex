
\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

In this section, we evaluate ApproxPilot-SE for approximate accelerator design and compare it with ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot,vlcek2025approxgnn}. We first examine the optimization results across different accelerators, including larger designs with substantially more configurable arithmetic sites. We then evaluate end-to-end data efficiency and investigate the effectiveness of iterative feedback, feedback selection, and latency modeling through ablation studies.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks:}
We evaluate ApproxPilot-SE on five domain-specific accelerators: DCT, Laplace filtering, Sobel edge detection, Gaussian filtering, and KMeans clustering. Their arithmetic operations and configurable sites are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}.

The DCT accelerator contains 80 arithmetic operations, including 76 configurable sites and four fixed exact operations. Laplace contains 60 configurable sites, while Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans contain 5, 17, and 16 sites, respectively. The initial design spaces of DCT and Laplace reach approximately $3.26\times10^{91}$ and $1.77\times10^{72}$ configurations, respectively. Compared with the accelerators evaluated in the original ApproxPilot framework~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot}, these larger accelerator structures substantially expand the combinatorial design space and present greater challenges for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Approximate operator library:}
The arithmetic-unit library combines EvoApproxLib components and CGP-generated approximate units. Each configurable site is assigned compatible arithmetic implementations according to operation type, bit width, signedness, and interface requirements. The resulting accelerator configurations exhibit different hardware costs and numerical error characteristics, providing the design space for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation platform:}
Hardware area, power, and latency are obtained using Synopsys Design Compiler with a 45-nm technology library. Application quality is evaluated using the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM)~\cite{wang2004ssim} on image-processing benchmarks, with evaluation images drawn from BSDS500~\cite{arbelaez2011contour}. Accuracy retention is calculated relative to the fully exact implementation.

\noindent\textbf{Framework settings:}
ApproxPilot-SE employs separate GNN models for PPA and application accuracy prediction. The PPA encoder contains four layers, while the accuracy encoder uses five layers. Both models have a hidden dimension of 128. NSGA-III is employed for multi-objective design space exploration, and local latency calibration uses a maximum Hamming distance of $d_{\max}=10$.

ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN serve as the baseline methods, retaining their respective prediction models and search strategies. In the primary DCT comparison, all three methods use 720 training configurations. ApproxPilot-SE begins with 540 configurations and incorporates two feedback rounds of 90 measurements each. Each framework selects 100 final candidates for ground-truth PPA and application-quality evaluation. Additional experiments use different training-label budgets to examine data efficiency.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metric:}
We employ three-dimensional PPA hypervolume (HV) gain to evaluate the optimization results under different SSIM-retention constraints. Area, power, and latency are normalized by the corresponding values of the exact accelerator. With reference point $\mathbf{r}$, HV gain is defined as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{HV}_{\mathrm{Gain}}
&=
\mathrm{HV}(\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
\cup\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})\\
&\quad-
\mathrm{HV}(\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hv_gain}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}=(1,1,1)$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\tau}$ contains the measured PPA points satisfying the SSIM-retention threshold $\tau$. A larger HV gain indicates better combined area, power, and latency tradeoffs beyond the exact implementation. All reported HV values are computed from ground-truth-verified designs.

\subsection{Overall Approximation Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization_results}


We first compare the final optimization results of ApproxPilot-SE with those of ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN. Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain} presents the verified PPA HV gains across five accelerators under 90\% and 99\% SSIM-retention requirements.

ApproxPilot-SE achieves the largest HV gain on all five benchmarks at both quality thresholds. On DCT, with 720 training configurations for each method, ApproxPilot-SE achieves an HV gain of 0.088623 under 90\% SSIM retention, compared with 0.023390 for ApproxPilot and 0.020672 for ApproxGNN. This corresponds to 3.79 times the gain of the stronger baseline.

The results on DCT and Laplace are particularly relevant to evaluating the framework on larger arithmetic configurations. At 90\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE achieves an HV gain of 0.211086 on Laplace, exceeding both baseline methods. Together with the DCT results, this demonstrates the effectiveness of the framework on accelerators containing substantially more configurable arithmetic sites than those considered in earlier evaluations.

The improvements also extend to Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans. Under 90\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE achieves HV gains of 0.096135, 0.243964, and 0.253267 on these benchmarks, respectively. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework is applicable across different computational structures and arithmetic-unit combinations.

Under the stricter 99\% SSIM-retention requirement, ApproxPilot-SE continues to achieve positive HV gains on all five benchmarks. In particular, the DCT HV gain remains at 0.064782, indicating that the framework can identify favorable hardware tradeoffs even when approximation is restricted by a high application-quality requirement.

Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency} compares the three methods on DCT using 540, 720, and 900 training configurations. The final design sets are evaluated using ground-truth PPA and SSIM, and their HV gains are calculated under the same 90\% SSIM-retention requirement.

ApproxPilot-SE achieves HV gains of 0.079690, 0.088623, and 0.090514 with 540, 720, and 900 training configurations, respectively, outperforming both baselines at every evaluated budget.

Notably, with only 540 training labels, ApproxPilot-SE achieves an HV gain of 0.079690, exceeding ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN even when they use 900 training labels. Compared with the stronger 900-label baseline, ApproxPilot-SE uses 40\% fewer training configurations while achieving 3.42 times the verified HV gain.

At 720 training labels, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.79 times the strongest baseline gain, and the advantage increases to 3.89 times at 900 labels. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework uses available measurement data more effectively and maintains its optimization advantage across different training budgets.

\noindent\textbf{Optimization time:}
We also examine the computational cost of the complete optimization process. For DCT with 720 training configurations, the estimated end-to-end runtimes are 4.47--6.00 h for ApproxPilot-SE, 4.08--5.74 h for ApproxPilot, and 5.65--8.07 h for ApproxGNN. These estimates indicate comparable overall execution-time ranges, while ApproxPilot-SE provides substantially higher verified HV gain. The result highlights the benefit of the proposed framework in utilizing measured design data within practical optimization costs.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

Each experiment modifies one component while retaining the remaining optimization procedure. All configurations use 720 training labels and undergo ground-truth evaluation of 100 final candidates. \noindent\textbf{Effect of iterative feedback:}
We first compare ApproxPilot-SE with a one-shot approach under the same training-label budget. The one-shot approach trains its surrogate models without incorporating search-selected measurements into subsequent model updates. In contrast, ApproxPilot-SE progressively evaluates selected configurations and retrains its models using the expanded training set.

The one-shot approach achieves a verified HV gain of 0.078411, while ApproxPilot-SE reaches 0.088623, representing an improvement of 13.02\%. The comparison demonstrates that iterative feedback provides additional optimization benefits beyond simply increasing the number of training labels. By allowing newly measured configurations to influence subsequent exploration, the framework achieves better final PPA tradeoffs.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of feedback selection:}
The effectiveness of iterative optimization also depends on which configurations are selected for measurement. To evaluate the proposed selection strategy, we replace Pareto-relevant and accuracy-boundary acquisition with random feedback while retaining the iterative model updates and measurement budget.

Random feedback achieves an HV gain of 0.076516, compared with 0.088623 for the complete framework. The resulting 15.82\% improvement demonstrates the value of targeted feedback selection. Prioritizing competitive predicted configurations while exploring the accuracy boundary allows the available measurements to contribute more effectively to subsequent design space exploration.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of local latency calibration:}
We next disable local latency calibration and directly use the latency predictions of the PPA surrogate. All other components remain unchanged.

Without calibration, the verified HV gain decreases to 0.073336. Enabling local calibration improves this result by 20.85\%, representing the largest relative improvement among the individual component-removal experiments.

This result shows that previously measured configurations provide useful references for evaluating new candidates. Incorporating measured latency information into the optimization process improves the final design selection, rather than merely reducing prediction errors in an offline evaluation.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of path-aware latency modeling:}
To evaluate path-aware readout, we replace timing-domain path aggregation with conventional global-max pooling while preserving the remaining framework. The global-max variant achieves an HV gain of 0.080654, whereas the complete framework reaches 0.088623, corresponding to a 9.88\% improvement.

The result indicates that incorporating arithmetic dependencies into the latency representation benefits the end-to-end optimization process. Unlike global-max pooling, path-aware aggregation retains information accumulated along computation paths, providing a more suitable representation for latency-related candidate comparisons.

We further replace the current latency prediction structure with the two-stage latency model used in ApproxPilot. This variant achieves an HV gain of 0.078382, while the complete ApproxPilot-SE framework improves the result by 13.07\%. Together, these comparisons support the effectiveness of the adopted latency modeling approach within the complete optimization workflow.

Overall, the ablation results demonstrate that iterative surrogate updates, targeted feedback acquisition, local latency calibration, and path-aware aggregation each contribute to the final measured optimization performance. The improvements are observed in ground-truth-verified accelerator designs, establishing their practical value for approximate design space exploration.
