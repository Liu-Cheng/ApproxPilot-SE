\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks:}
We evaluate ApproxPilot-SE on five domain-specific accelerators: DCT, Laplace filtering, Sobel edge detection, Gaussian filtering, and KMeans clustering. Their arithmetic operations and configurable sites are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}.

The DCT accelerator contains 80 arithmetic operations, including 76 configurable sites and four fixed exact operations. Laplace contains 60 configurable sites, while Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans contain 5, 17, and 16 sites, respectively. The initial design spaces of DCT and Laplace reach approximately $3.26\times10^{91}$ and $1.77\times10^{72}$ configurations, respectively. Compared with the accelerators evaluated in the original ApproxPilot framework~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot}, these larger accelerator structures substantially expand the combinatorial design space and present greater challenges for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Approximate operator library:}
The arithmetic-unit library combines EvoApproxLib components and CGP-generated approximate units. Each configurable site is assigned compatible arithmetic implementations according to operation type, bit width, signedness, and interface requirements. The resulting accelerator configurations exhibit different hardware costs and numerical error characteristics, providing the design space for system-level approximation optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation platform:}
Hardware area, power, and latency are obtained using Synopsys Design Compiler with a 45-nm technology library. Application quality is evaluated using the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM)~\cite{wang2004ssim} on image-processing benchmarks, with evaluation images drawn from BSDS500~\cite{arbelaez2011contour}. Accuracy retention is calculated relative to the fully exact implementation.

\noindent\textbf{Framework settings:}
ApproxPilot-SE employs separate GNN models for PPA and application accuracy prediction. The PPA encoder contains four layers, while the accuracy encoder uses five layers. Both models have a hidden dimension of 128. NSGA-III is employed for multi-objective design space exploration, and local latency calibration uses a maximum Hamming distance of $d_{\max}=10$.







ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN serve as the baseline methods, retaining their respective prediction models and search strategies. For DCT, we adopt a full training budget of 900 labeled configurations, with an additional 100 configurations reserved for validation. To evaluate data efficiency, we consider 60\%, 80\%, and 100\% of the full training budget, corresponding to 540, 720, and 900 configurations, respectively. ApproxPilot-SE starts with 540 initial configurations and progressively expands its training set through search-selected feedback, adding 90 measured configurations per round. After optimization, each method selects 100 final candidates for independent ground-truth PPA and application-quality evaluation.


\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metric:}
We employ three-dimensional PPA hypervolume (HV) gain to evaluate the optimization results under different SSIM-retention constraints. Area, power, and latency are normalized by the corresponding values of the exact accelerator. With reference point $\mathbf{r}$, HV gain is defined as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{HV}_{\mathrm{Gain}}
&=
\mathrm{HV}(\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
\cup\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r})\\
&\quad-
\mathrm{HV}(\{\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}\};\mathbf{r}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hv_gain}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{ex}}=(1,1,1)$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\tau}$ contains the measured PPA points satisfying the SSIM-retention threshold $\tau$. A larger HV gain indicates better combined area, power, and latency tradeoffs beyond the exact implementation. All reported HV values are computed from ground-truth-verified designs.











\subsection{Overall Approximation Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization_results}

We first compare ApproxPilot-SE with ApproxPilot and ApproxGNN across five accelerators under SSIM-retention requirements ranging from 80\% to 99\%. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain}, ApproxPilot-SE achieves the highest or equally highest verified PPA HV Gain in 24 of the 25 evaluated cases, demonstrating its effectiveness across different accelerator structures and quality constraints.

The advantages are particularly evident on the larger DCT and Laplace benchmarks. At 90\% SSIM retention, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.79$\times$ and 2.39$\times$ the HV Gain of the strongest baseline on DCT and Laplace, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in exploring substantially larger combinatorial design spaces. Consistent improvements are also observed on Sobel, Gaussian, and KMeans, indicating its applicability across different arithmetic structures.

As the SSIM-retention requirement becomes more stringent, the feasible design space becomes increasingly restricted, making it more challenging to obtain favorable hardware tradeoffs. Nevertheless, ApproxPilot-SE maintains a substantial advantage in the high-accuracy region. On DCT, its improvement over the strongest baseline increases from 2.84$\times$ at 80\% retention to 4.65$\times$ at 95\% retention. Even at 99\% retention, ApproxPilot-SE maintains a clear advantage and achieves the highest HV Gain on all five benchmarks. These results highlight its ability to identify hardware-efficient configurations while preserving high application accuracy.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:measured_pareto}, ApproxPilot-SE achieves more favorable Pareto fronts across all three hardware metrics. Compared with the baseline methods, its solutions extend toward lower area, power, and delay over a broad range of SSIM levels. This improvement indicates that ApproxPilot-SE can identify a wider range of competitive hardware configurations rather than obtaining gains at only a few operating points. Consequently, designers can select different implementations according to their hardware constraints and application-quality requirements.

The advantage is particularly important in the high-SSIM region, where approximation opportunities are more limited. While aggressive approximation can achieve hardware savings at the expense of output quality, maintaining high SSIM requires more selective arithmetic-unit substitutions. ApproxPilot-SE continues to identify favorable PPA--quality tradeoffs in this region, demonstrating its ability to balance hardware efficiency and application accuracy. Together with the HV results in Table~\ref{tab:hv_gain}, these observations show that ApproxPilot-SE improves both the overall quality of the explored design set and the available hardware tradeoffs under stringent accuracy requirements.

\noindent\textbf{End-to-End Data Efficiency:}
While the preceding results demonstrate the optimization quality of ApproxPilot-SE, the efficiency of obtaining these results is equally important. System-level synthesis and application-quality evaluation are computationally expensive, making the effective utilization of measured training data essential for large-scale approximate accelerator optimization. To investigate this aspect, Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency} compares the three frameworks using 60\%, 80\%, and 100\% of the full training budget, corresponding to 540, 720, and 900 configurations, respectively.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{end_to_end_sample_efficiency.pdf}
\caption{End-to-end data efficiency on DCT under 90\% SSIM retention. HV Gain is computed from ground-truth-verified final designs.}
\label{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}
\end{figure}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:end_to_end_sample_efficiency}, ApproxPilot-SE consistently achieves substantially higher verified HV Gain than both baselines across all three training budgets. Remarkably, using only 60\% of the available training labels, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 3.42$\times$ the HV Gain of the strongest baseline using the full training budget. This result highlights the ability of ApproxPilot-SE to identify competitive accelerator configurations with considerably fewer ground-truth measurements, rather than relying on larger training datasets to obtain superior optimization results.

The advantage is also maintained under equal training budgets, reaching 3.79$\times$ and 3.89$\times$ the strongest baseline at 720 and 900 training labels, respectively. Moreover, increasing the training budget from 540 to 720 improves the HV Gain of ApproxPilot-SE by 11.2\%, whereas a further increase to 900 provides an additional 2.1\% improvement. This trend indicates that substantial optimization benefits can already be obtained with a reduced training budget, with progressively smaller gains from additional measurements over the evaluated range. By incorporating search-selected configurations into subsequent surrogate updates, ApproxPilot-SE makes effective use of the available measurements to guide the exploration process.

Beyond measurement efficiency, we also compare the overall optimization time. With 720 training configurations, the estimated end-to-end runtimes of ApproxPilot-SE, ApproxPilot, and ApproxGNN are 4.47--6.00 h, 4.08--5.74 h, and 5.65--8.07 h, respectively. ApproxPilot-SE exhibits a runtime range comparable to ApproxPilot and generally lower than ApproxGNN, while achieving significantly higher verified HV Gain. These results demonstrate that ApproxPilot-SE improves the quality of the final optimized designs without requiring a substantial increase in overall optimization time.

\noindent\textbf{Iterative Feedback and Selection:}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:e2e_ablation}, ApproxPilot-SE achieves 13.02\% higher verified HV Gain than one-shot optimization under the same training budget. This improvement confirms the benefit of integrating surrogate updates into DSE rather than separating model training from the search process. Measurements acquired during exploration allow subsequent surrogate models to reflect the design regions encountered by the search, improving the final optimization outcome.

The selection of feedback configurations is equally important. Replacing the proposed selection strategy with random acquisition reduces the optimization performance, with ApproxPilot-SE achieving a 15.82\% higher HV Gain. This comparison shows that the benefit of feedback depends not only on updating the models, but also on selecting configurations relevant to the optimization objectives. Together, these results establish the effectiveness of the proposed co-evolution between surrogate learning and design space exploration.

\noindent\textbf{Latency Modeling:}
The remaining ablations examine how latency modeling affects the final PPA optimization. Among the evaluated component removals, disabling local Delay calibration causes the largest reduction in verified HV Gain. The complete framework achieves a 20.85\% improvement over the uncalibrated variant, demonstrating the practical value of incorporating measured reference configurations into latency estimation. This improvement also shows that local calibration benefits the actual selection of accelerator designs, beyond its effect on prediction accuracy.

Path-aware readout provides a further improvement of 9.88\% over conventional global-max pooling. The result supports incorporating computation-path dependencies into the latency representation, rather than relying solely on graph-level pooling. Furthermore, replacing the proposed Delay model with the two-stage architecture of ApproxPilot reduces the final HV Gain, with the complete framework achieving a 13.07\% advantage. These comparisons demonstrate that both local calibration and path-aware latency modeling contribute to identifying more competitive hardware configurations.

Overall, the ablation studies confirm that the optimization gains of ApproxPilot-SE arise from the combination of iterative surrogate updates, guided measurement selection, and hardware-aware latency estimation. Their contributions are reflected directly in verified system-level optimization results.
