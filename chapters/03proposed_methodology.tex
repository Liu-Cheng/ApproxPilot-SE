\section{ApproxPilot-SE Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/ApproxPilot_SE_Framework.pdf}
    \caption{Overview of ApproxPilot-SE. Co-evolution couples candidate generation, PPA/SSIM prediction, local Delay calibration, and ground-truth feedback. Final DSE uses the updated models and references to identify designs for real verification and Pareto filtering.}
    \label{fig:framework}
\end{figure*}

ApproxPilot-SE automates the selection of approximate arithmetic units under a limited ground-truth evaluation budget. Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} shows its two stages. Surrogate co-evolution acquires informative measurements and updates the models used to guide exploration. Final design space exploration (DSE) then uses the resulting models and measured references to identify optimized designs for verification. The surrogate structures and local Delay calibration are developed in Section~\ref{sec:surrogate_modeling}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Framework Overview}
\label{subsec:problem_overview}

Given the RTL description of a target accelerator and an arithmetic-unit library, let $N$ denote the number of configurable arithmetic sites.
For each site $i$, let $e_i$ denote its exact implementation and $\mathcal{A}_i$ the set of available approximate implementations.
The legal implementation set at each site and the complete configuration space are defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{C}_i = \{e_i\} \cup \mathcal{A}_i,
    \qquad
    \Omega = \prod_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{C}_i.
    \label{eq:se_configuration_space}
\end{equation}
A candidate design is represented by a configuration vector $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in\Omega$, where $x_i$ specifies the implementation assigned to site $i$.
Exact and approximate implementations are treated as alternatives within the same configuration space and selected jointly through system-level optimization.

For a configuration $\mathbf{x}$, let $A(\mathbf{x})$, $P(\mathbf{x})$, and $D(\mathbf{x})$ denote its system-level area, power, and delay, respectively, obtained under a common synthesis setup.
Let $Q(\mathbf{x})$ denote mean application-level SSIM over a fixed set of evaluation inputs, with larger values indicating higher output accuracy.
These metrics are obtained through logic synthesis and application-level execution and constitute the ground-truth evaluation of the configuration.
Let $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{ex}}$ denote the fully exact configuration, with corresponding metrics $A_{\mathrm{ex}}$, $P_{\mathrm{ex}}$, $D_{\mathrm{ex}}$, and $Q_{\mathrm{ex}}$.
Using the fully exact design as the PPA normalization reference, the four-objective optimization problem is formulated as
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}\;
    \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
    =
    \left(
        \frac{A(\mathbf{x})}{A_{\mathrm{ex}}},
        \frac{P(\mathbf{x})}{P_{\mathrm{ex}}},
        \frac{D(\mathbf{x})}{D_{\mathrm{ex}}},
        1-Q(\mathbf{x})
    \right).
    \label{eq:se_search_objectives}
\end{equation}
All four objectives are minimized, with the last term corresponding to the maximization of output quality.
During search, surrogate estimates of these metrics are used to compare candidates and explore tradeoffs between hardware cost and output quality.
The cost of logic synthesis and application-level evaluation limits the number of configurations for which ground-truth labels can be obtained.
We measure the evaluation budget by the number of distinct configurations evaluated.
Let $N_{\mathrm{init}}$, $N_{\mathrm{fb}}$, and $N_{\mathrm{ver}}$ denote the numbers of newly evaluated configurations during initialization, search feedback, and final verification, respectively.
The budget constraint is
\begin{equation}
    N_{\mathrm{init}}
    +
    N_{\mathrm{fb}}
    +
    N_{\mathrm{ver}}
    \le B,
    \label{eq:se_evaluation_budget}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the ground-truth evaluation budget for the target system.
Surrogate predictions enable rapid comparison of large numbers of candidates, while ground-truth evaluations provide search feedback and verify the final designs.
The optimization process thus determines both the arithmetic-unit assignments and which candidates receive ground-truth evaluation.

The two stages share this design space and objective definition but serve different purposes. During co-evolution, search identifies configurations whose measurements extend the training data and improve subsequent predictions. During final DSE, the updated predictors and calibration references are fixed, and search identifies the final hardware alternatives. Ground-truth synthesis and application execution connect both stages to the same target accelerator.

\subsection{Surrogate Co-Evolution}
\label{subsec:search_feedback}

The co-evolution stage couples surrogate learning with search-guided data acquisition. An initial set of distinct legal configurations is sampled from $\Omega$ and evaluated by logic synthesis and application-level SSIM computation. Their measured labels form the initial training set $\mathcal{L}_0$. The PPA and SSIM surrogates are trained on this set, and its measured configurations initialize the local-calibration reference set $\mathcal{R}_0$. The initial samples are selected without using their unknown system-level labels.

At active feedback round $t$, the current surrogates and calibration references provide the four-objective estimate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_t(\mathbf{x})=
\left[
\frac{\widehat A_t(\mathbf{x})}{A_{\mathrm{ex}}},
\frac{\widehat P_t(\mathbf{x})}{P_{\mathrm{ex}}},
\frac{\widetilde D_t(\mathbf{x})}{D_{\mathrm{ex}}},
1-\widehat Q_t(\mathbf{x})
\right],
\label{eq:round_objectives}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde D_t$ is the locally calibrated Delay estimate defined in Section~\ref{subsec:local_calibration}. Area, Power, and SSIM retain their direct surrogate predictions. The Exact configuration and current training configurations seed the search pool. Surrogate-guided NSGA-III then generates and compares legal configurations using Eq.~\ref{eq:round_objectives}. Its internal population updates use nondominated sorting and reference-direction niching to retain diverse objective tradeoffs. The models and calibration references remain fixed throughout this search call.

Feedback acquisition operates on the unique, unevaluated configurations explored by that call. Candidates meeting the prescribed predicted-quality requirement are ordered by nondomination rank. If a front contains more candidates than the remaining evaluation quota, larger crowding distance gives higher selection priority. A portion of the quota is also reserved for quality-boundary candidates, whose predicted SSIM lies just below the acquisition threshold; those closest to the threshold are selected first. The threshold guides measurement acquisition while the search itself retains the four-objective formulation. Thus, crowding-distance selection determines which configurations receive measurements after a search call, separately from NSGA-III's internal population survival.

Let $\mathcal S_t$ be the selected feedback set and $\mathbf y(\mathbf{x})=(A(\mathbf{x}),P(\mathbf{x}),D(\mathbf{x}),Q(\mathbf{x}))$ its measured system-level label. Logic synthesis and application execution produce
\begin{equation}
\mathcal Y_t=
\left\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf y(\mathbf{x}))\mid
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal S_t\right\}.
\label{eq:new_feedback_labels}
\end{equation}
The cumulative training set is updated as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{t+1}=\mathcal L_t\cup\mathcal Y_t.
\label{eq:training_update}
\end{equation}
Both surrogate groups are retrained on the expanded measured dataset. The newly measured training configurations also enter the calibration reference set. After the model update, predictions at all references are recomputed and their Delay residuals are refreshed, keeping the correction consistent with the current surrogate.

The next active feedback round uses these updated models and references to collect further candidates. The round index $t$ denotes this outer acquisition--measurement--retraining cycle; one round contains a complete search call with multiple population generations. Search therefore guides the acquisition of labels, and the resulting labels improve the predictors and local corrections that guide the next search. Algorithm~\ref{alg:search_feedback} summarizes this interaction and the transition to final DSE.

\subsection{Final Design Space Exploration}
\label{subsec:final_dse}

After co-evolution, the final PPA and SSIM surrogates and their measured reference set are held fixed. Final DSE uses Eq.~\ref{eq:round_objectives} with these predictors and local Delay corrections to explore the legal design space. It can use the same NSGA-III search procedure as the acquisition stage, but its objective is to obtain the final design alternatives rather than another training batch. No model update is performed during this search.

Distinct final candidates are selected from the predicted search archive by nondomination rank and crowding distance and evaluated through logic synthesis and application-level SSIM computation. The resulting measurements, together with the known Exact reference, determine the verified design set. Predicted objectives guide candidate selection; measured PPA and SSIM determine the reported frontier.

Let $\mathcal{V}$ denote the set of final candidate configurations evaluated through logic synthesis and application-level execution.
For a required quality-retention ratio $\tau\in(0,1]$, the feasible PPA-nondominated subset is extracted as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\tau}
    =
    \operatorname{ND}_{A,P,D}
    \left(
        \left\{
            \mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{V}
            \;\middle|\;
            \frac{Q(\mathbf{x})}{Q_{\mathrm{ex}}}\ge\tau
        \right\}
    \right),
    \label{eq:se_feasible_pareto}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\mathrm{ex}}>0$, and $\operatorname{ND}_{A,P,D}$ denotes nondominated filtering based on measured area, power, and delay.
A design dominates another if none of its three PPA metrics is larger and at least one is strictly smaller.
The feasible designs in $\mathcal{V}$ that are not dominated by any other feasible design in this set constitute $\mathcal{P}_{\tau}$.
The same evaluated candidate set can therefore provide PPA tradeoffs under different output-quality requirements.

The output is a Verified Pareto Front under the specified quality requirement, from which representative designs can be selected according to the desired hardware tradeoff.
