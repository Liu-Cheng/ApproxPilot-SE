\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{pproximate} computing exploits the error tolerance of applications to trade off output quality against hardware cost~\cite{jiang2020approxarith,que2023approxsurvey,xu2016approxsurvey}.
For accelerators with multiple replaceable arithmetic units, combining approximate implementations at different locations creates a large number of candidate designs.
However, evaluating each candidate through logic synthesis and application-level assessment is computationally expensive.
Prior studies therefore use surrogate models to replace many of these evaluations and improve the efficiency of approximate design space exploration (DSE).
AutoAx~\cite{mrazek2019autoax} uses machine learning to predict the hardware cost and output quality of candidate designs, treating an accelerator composed of approximate arithmetic units as a black-box function.
ApproxPilot~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot} introduces graph neural networks (GNNs) to improve prediction accuracy by modeling circuit topology and inferring critical-path information.
ApproxGNN~\cite{vlcek2025approxgnn} enables model transfer by learning representations of approximate components and demonstrates the reuse of pretrained models across image convolution filters.
These methods have substantially advanced surrogate-assisted approximate design.
Building reliable surrogates for target circuits without suitable pretrained models requires large numbers of ground-truth labels.
Constructing the training set of approximately 4,000 configurations for AutoAx required about 11 hours~\cite{mrazek2019autoax}.
ApproxPilot used 105,000 design samples for each of the Gaussian and K-means benchmarks~\cite{zhang2025approxpilot}, while the initial ApproxGNN dataset contained 15,900 synthesized configurations~\cite{vlcek2025approxgnn}.
Reducing the evaluation cost of surrogate training and enabling limited ground-truth data to contribute throughout search are therefore key challenges in improving the efficiency of approximate accelerator design optimization.

With few ground-truth labels, surrogate prediction bias can affect candidate comparison and selection during multiobjective search.
The cited approximate-accelerator approaches primarily use ground-truth results for offline surrogate training~\cite{mrazek2019autoax,zhang2025approxpilot,vlcek2025approxgnn}.
Evaluated reference designs also provide local measurement anchors for nearby candidates in the configuration space.
For candidates with similar configurations, local calibration combines the measured reference delay with the surrogate-predicted delay difference between the candidate and the reference.
This allows existing synthesis results to directly correct candidate predictions.
Periodic evaluation of promising search candidates supplies labels for surrogate updates.
The former mechanism incorporates existing measurements into candidate prediction, whereas the latter enables the surrogates to evolve during search.
Together, they couple prediction and search under a limited evaluation budget.

Building on this idea, we propose \textbf{ApproxPilot-SE}, a framework for optimizing approximate accelerators under a limited budget for ground-truth evaluations.
The framework first uses a small set of evaluated designs to train surrogates for power, performance, and area (PPA) and output quality.
It incorporates path-aware aggregation to capture structural dependencies relevant to delay.
During surrogate-assisted multiobjective DSE, ApproxPilot-SE locally calibrates candidate delay predictions using nearby evaluated configurations.
It also periodically evaluates promising candidates and uses the resulting ground-truth labels to update the surrogates and guide subsequent search.
Finally, logic synthesis and application-level quality evaluation are used to construct a validated Pareto front.
ApproxPilot-SE reuses evaluation data as training labels and local prediction references throughout the search, linking measured feedback directly to subsequent design optimization.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{We develop a configuration-aware PPA surrogate with a dedicated path-aware Delay readout and measured-reference local calibration.}
    Timing-domain path aggregation captures delay dependencies, while nearby measured configurations correct local Delay prediction bias.

    \item \textbf{We establish a closed-loop co-evolution mechanism that tightly couples surrogate learning with multi-objective DSE.}
    Pareto-guided exploration identifies candidates for ground-truth evaluation, whose measured outcomes update the PPA and SSIM surrogates and their local calibration references.

    \item \textbf{Extensive experiments across multiple domain-specific accelerators demonstrate the effectiveness of ApproxPilot-SE.}
    On the discrete cosine transform (DCT) benchmark, ApproxPilot-SE achieves $3.81\times$ the strongest baseline's verified 3D PPA HV Gain at 90\% SSIM retention, using 720 training configurations versus 900.
\end{enumerate}
